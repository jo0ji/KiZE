Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

Introduction: Through the lectures, I learned 4 ethical issues: misuse, dual-use, fairness, and trust. To demonstrate my ability to identify ethical issues and their societal implications, I analyzed a case study regarding dual-use technology and fairness in AI access.

Case: The Protein Folding Breakthrough (Biotechnology)

A university released ProteoGen, an AI that predicts protein folding to help with medical research. However, a startup secretly changed it to make toxins resistant to human immune systems and marketed it to clients. Additionally, running the AI requires massive computing power, meaning only wealthy institutions can afford it, leaving out researchers in poorer countries.

This case perfectly illustrates the dual-use dilemma, indicating that helpful technology can easily be weaponized. It also highlights a lack of fairness regarding equitable access to life-saving research tools. The societal implications are that evil people can exploit AI in a bad way to easily create dangerous bioweapons. The lack of fairness in access also widens the global health inequality gap.

Even though the creators believe the AI can become helpful to save many people, the risk of dual-use can not be avoidable. Furthermore, calling a tool "open-source" lacks fairness if massive financial barriers prevent global researchers from actually using it.
